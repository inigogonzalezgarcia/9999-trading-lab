# Data specification

CSV and Parquet require `timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume`. V1 prefers a nonnegative finite `spread_pips` value on every executable candle. If absent, execution may use only the explicitly configured `spread.fixed_fallback_pips`; if neither exists, the candidate is retained as `SPREAD_DATA_UNAVAILABLE` and excluded from executable trades. Timestamps must include a timezone or an explicit IANA timezone setting on import. Mixed aware and naive values are rejected. Naive local timestamps on DST gaps/folds are rejected rather than guessed. Internally timestamps are UTC candle-close times; `config/data.yaml` records whether source timestamps denote bar open or close. Duplicate normalized timestamps and invalid/non-finite OHLCV rows are rejected. Out-of-order rows are reported and sorted. Missing intervals and weekend-dated bars are reported, never filled. Dataset identity is a SHA-256 of source bytes.

Resampling is UTC-aligned, right-closed with OHLC first/max/min/last and volume sum; labels are bar close time. The close timestamp is the earliest availability time for a completed candle. Causal consumers must filter to timestamps at or before the decision instant. No synthetic data is added.


Canonical files are produced by `python -m backend.cli convert` / `fetch` (see `backend/app/ingest.py`): HistData generic ASCII is interpreted as EST without DST and converted to UTC; Dukascopy exports are GMT; the Dukascopy tick feed is aggregated to bid M1 bars with the per-minute median spread in pips. Files carrying a `dataset_classification` column equal to `SYNTHETIC_STRESS_DATA` are synthetic and must never be reported as research evidence.

When the machine running the shell has no network, hourly `.bi5` files can be fetched by a browser and saved as a *bundle*: repeated records of `SYMBOL/YYYY/MM0/DD/HHh_ticks.bi5 <length>\n` followed by the raw bytes (zero-based month like Dukascopy's paths; zero length = no data that hour). `python -m backend.cli convert bundle` decodes bundles into the canonical CSV. Dukascopy throttles aggressive clients with HTTP 503: keep concurrency at 2 with ~100 ms pauses and exponential back-off.
